What the desk should know

The original incident page says that one parser fix covers every report. Its rendered cause table contradicts that claim. The export contains two distinct mechanisms, so the desk needs separate tickets and resolutions.

The public tickets are shared date parser and booking-v3 date hint.

This is a review of the export dated 4 October 2026, not a live view of the desk. The desk can receive and merge more reports while this review is being written. The proposed rule below replaces decision 0001 only after the desk accepts it.

Engineering update after the initial review

The course desk update reports that #4471 closed on 5 October:

  • The parser was fixed: fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales.
  • The hint was marked won’t fix because the booking-v3 form was retired.

The parser ticket is therefore completed; the hint ticket is not planned. Retiring the form is not evidence that its hint was repaired. These are reported engineering outcomes, not fixes independently tested against production by this review. Shared date parser

The shared parser treats input as month first even when the tenant writes day first. An app or email report from a day-first tenant belongs here; a form report using booking-v2 does too. For example, Harbour Dental’s app report R-41200 says that 7 April became 4 July.

The parser issue should establish an explicit input-date contract and verify day/month handling across affected locales and channels. It must not be closed just because the booking-v3 hint is corrected.

Booking-v3 date hint

Booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY, but its form reads the input in the tenant’s day-first order. A person following the hint therefore gets a different date. These are form reports from Alder Physio, Copper Studio and Pale Harbour. R-41230 is an example: Copper Studio uses booking-v3 and es-ES, despite the report’s en-GB browser locale and parser intake tag.

The hint issue should make the displayed format match the form’s actual input contract and verify that following the hint preserves the intended date. Changing only the shared parser does not resolve this separate UI contract.

Reports requiring follow-up

The table’s one-day-move footnote is evidence of uncertainty, not a confirmed third cause. The desk notes identify R-41202, R-41209, R-41236, R-41344 and R-41620. Their bodies describe one-day or temporal anomalies, but the exported classifier places them under parser from tenant and channel alone.

Keep that limitation visible on the parser ticket. Ask for the original input, expected and actual dates, timezone and processing trace before asserting a timezone bug or changing the allocation. Do not silently drop these reports or double-count them in another ticket.

When the reports arrived

The received and merged bars are different events. Reports arrived before the July merge backlog was processed and continued afterwards. A merge spike does not establish an arrival spike, a new regression date or a completed fix.

Proposed replacement for subject-only merging

This is a new decision record. The accepted decision 0001 is preserved unchanged. On acceptance, use this record instead of the old subject-only triage rule.

  1. Read the report body and look up its tenant, locale, form version and channel.
  2. Compare the observed failure and mechanism with the candidate ticket.
  3. Merge only when the evidence supports the same cause. Matching subjects alone are insufficient; different subjects alone do not rule out a duplicate.
  4. If the tenant is unknown, the evidence conflicts or the mechanism is unclear, keep the report separate and request the missing facts.
  5. Count each report ID once. Preserve report IDs, receipt times and merge times so another reviewer can reproduce the split.
